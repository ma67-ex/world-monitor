What is World Monitor?

World Monitor is a free ($0), open-source (AGPL-3.0) dashboard used by 2M+ people. It streams 966 news and OSINT feeds from 1379 attributed providers onto one live world map with 61 map layer types, spanning geopolitics, markets, shipping, aviation and cyber. It correlates them so converging events surface before the headlines do. Every figure is sourced and every method documented.

Best for analysts, journalists, traders and builders who need live geopolitical signals.

World Monitor is not an open-source Palantir

Satellite tracking

Live orbital positions computed in-browser with SGP4 and updated every 3 seconds — military and Earth-observation satellites from CelesTrak, refreshed every 2 hours.

GPS jamming zones

Daily RF-interference map from aircraft reports: cells where at least 2% of aircraft see degraded GPS are flagged, over 10% marked high. The cells are hexagons rebuilt once a day from those reports, so the map shows where flights met jamming or spoofing over the latest day, not this minute.

Chokepoint transit intel

Vessel counts through Hormuz, Suez, Bab el-Mandeb and 10 more straits — with disruption scoring.

AI datacenter map

313 AI datacenters mapped with power and operator metadata — the physical layer of the AI race.

Submarine cables

98 undersea cables that carry the internet, overlaid with outage and threat signals.

Protest tracking

Protest detection from GDELT; reports from one source in the same 0.1° cell on the same day merge into one event — signal, not rumor.

Don't take the numbers on faith — open the app and count. Sources are cited on every panel.

What are common questions about World Monitor?

12 straight answers on price, data, alerts and AI access. The short version: the dashboard costs $0, needs no account, and the code is open source under AGPL-3.0.

Is World Monitor really free?
Yes. The live map costs $0 with no signup and no trial clock: 61 map layer types, every one but Resilience, plus curated feeds, country briefs, breaking alerts and monitoring surfaces. Pro pays for the feeds: it adds WM Analyst, the scenario engine, Route Explorer, scheduled digests, MCP access for AI clients and the Resilience map layer. No ads, and no account means there's no user data to sell.
What is a global intelligence dashboard?
A single live view that fuses signals most tools keep separate: conflict events, ship and aircraft tracking, shipping chokepoints, satellites, subsea cables, pipelines, markets and cyber threats — on one real-time world map, with AI scoring and briefs on top. World Monitor is a free one.
Where does the data come from?
From 1379 attributed providers. Named ones include GDELT and UCDP for conflict, AISStream for live vessels, OpenSky for aircraft, NASA FIRMS for fires, USGS for earthquakes, IMF, BIS, FRED and Finnhub for markets and macro — plus curated news feeds. Every panel cites its sources and timestamps inline.
Can I watch chokepoints like Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb in real time?
Yes. 13 chokepoints, both straits and canals, from Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb to Suez, are tracked with live AIS vessel counts, week-over-week transit change and disruption scoring — and density anomalies are flagged against each chokepoint's rolling baseline.
How is this different from a conflict map like Liveuamap?
Conflict is one map layer. World Monitor fuses conflicts with shipping, military flights, infrastructure, markets and cyber — then AI scores the convergence: instability indices, hotspot escalation, corroborated breaking alerts and daily briefs. Compare World Monitor with Liveuamap and other conflict maps: worldmonitor.app/compare/liveuamap-alternatives. For broader monitoring options, see worldmonitor.app/compare/best-geopolitical-risk-dashboards.
Why should I trust an alert?
Because a banner has to clear fixed gates. It fires for news items classified as a critical or high threat, and for official civil-defense sirens. A news alert must be under 15 minutes old, the same story is held back for 30 minutes, and a 60-second cooldown follows any alert unless the new one is more severe. Keyword-only matches from lower-tier outlets also need AI confirmation. Sirens skip the age and cooldown gates because they are time-critical.
Can AI agents like Claude or GPT use it?
Yes. World Monitor's MCP server exposes 100 live tools, so Claude, GPT or any MCP-compatible agent can query risk scores, chokepoint status, conflicts, markets and briefs — with JMESPath projection so agents fetch exactly what they need.
Do I need an account?
No. The dashboard works instantly with no signup. An account only matters when you upgrade to Pro or want settings synced across devices.
Is it open source?
Yes — open source under AGPL-3.0 on GitHub: read the code, self-host it, or build on it. Pro and Pro Business include desktop keys, so your own desktop build can load Pro cloud data; Docker copies run on their own data. Native desktop apps for Windows, macOS and Linux are available too, plus an Android TV app for wall displays under an Enterprise contract.
Why isn't World Monitor Palantir?
Palantir makes an institution's own data operational inside that institution. World Monitor starts from the other end: it assembles, normalizes, correlates, and monitors public-world data, then publishes that intelligence layer through a free dashboard and open-source code. Different job, different product.
Which World Monitor license do I need?
Choose based on who will use the output. Pro's Personal license is for one person using World Monitor for their own research and decisions. Pro Business lets one licensed user use it for company or client work, internal tools, and reports. API Starter covers API use inside one organization, with no customer redistribution. API Business lets that organization embed World Monitor data in products and services for its customers. Enterprise covers custom seats, white-labeling, and deployments. These subscription licenses are separate from the AGPL/MIT source-code licenses. Full terms: worldmonitor.app/docs/terms.
What is the best software for tracking global news disruptions?
World Monitor is a free, open-source option: it streams 966 news and OSINT feeds from 1379 attributed providers onto one live world map with 61 map layer types, spanning geopolitics, markets, shipping, aviation and cyber. It correlates them so converging disruptions surface before the headlines do. To weigh it against other tools, see worldmonitor.app/compare/best-geopolitical-risk-dashboards.
